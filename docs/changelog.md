## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Six more demo captures, and one film (`tooling/marketing`, `docs/demo-script.md`).** `inbox-ai-reply` had the capture rig to itself; there are now seven clips covering the argument end to end — `onboard-invite` (a book escaping Instagram DMs), `profile-builds-itself` (an agent proposing a fact and quoting the sentence it heard), `match-board`, `candidate-view`, `realtime-two-up` and `admin-ai-cost` — plus `pnpm stitch:demo`, which joins the rendered clips into a single 1:40 mp4, and a narration script written against that running order. *Why:* the hackathon submission needs one video under three minutes, and seven short takes plus an edit is the only shape where fixing a beat in the last scene does not mean re-filming the first six.
  - **`Director` can film a companion page.** `realtime-two-up` has to show a candidate's phone and a matchmaker's laptop moving *together*; filmed separately it would demonstrate nothing, since the claim is simultaneity. The companion is photographed immediately after the main page at every beat under the same frame name, and `render-demo.mjs` pastes the pair side by side before anything else runs — so the concat list, the `xfade` expansion and the GIF palette all still see one directory of numbered stills. A dark gutter between the halves is load-bearing: butted together, two takes of the same app in the same palette read as one window with an extra column.
  - **A capture now empties the database first (`lib/scene.ts`, `harness/reset.ts`).** Captures name their people explicitly, because the names are on camera and have to stay the same across clips; that throws away the namespacing the e2e suite relies on, so two captures asking for `sam.okonkwo@example.com` both got a `users` row and `signInAs` kept resolving the first — who owned neither book. Every capture but the first timed out on a "Page not found", but *only when run together*, which is why each one passed on its own. `seedScene` resets to the baseline fixture before seeding, which is safe precisely because captures are scenes that run one at a time and assert nothing about each other. A spec must never do this.
  - **The film is cut as one afternoon, not seven features.** The first version joined the clips with dissolves and played as seven demos in a row — nothing carried between them, and the matchmaker on screen was never introduced. The narration now names Maya in its first eight words, over the shot of her own book, and follows her through one stretch of work, with each scene opening on the phrase that joins it to the last. *Why it needed fixing in the edit:* the clips were never the problem; the film had no connective tissue and no pauses.
  - **A title card, and a silent card before every scene (`renderFrameCards`).** Both come out of `docs/demo-script.md` — a `## Title card` blockquote and a `**Card:**` line per scene — and each becomes a segment in its own right rather than something drawn over a clip. The picture *stops* for about two seconds, which is the punctuation the first cut was missing. The film is 1:58.
  - **The film carries its own subtitles, read out of the narration script.** `stitch-demo.mjs` parses the blockquotes in `docs/demo-script.md`, cuts them into cards of at most fourteen words and gives each a share of its clip's running time proportional to word count — so a card is on screen for about as long as it takes to say, and the film doubles as a teleprompter. A matching `.srt` is written beside the mp4, and `--no-subtitles` renders it clean. *Why the cards are drawn in Chromium:* this machine's ffmpeg is built without libass **and** without libfreetype, so `subtitles` and `drawtext` are both unavailable and `overlay` is the only way text reaches the picture — and the browser is the better typesetter anyway.
  - **Subtitles sit in a band under the picture rather than over it**, and the film plays at **0.95×**. The band is not decoration: this app keeps what a demo is about at the *bottom* of the screen, so an overlaid caption covered the suggestion card's own quote and its Accept button — the payoff of a whole clip — and a Send button in two others. The slowdown is five percent, because the clips were paced to be watched and the film is meant to be narrated over.
  - **`admin-ai-cost` drives `apps/admin`**, so `playwright.config.ts` grew a second project matched on the `admin-` filename prefix. Without it the capture would inherit the app's `baseURL` and photograph a 404.
- **A mark of our own, and every icon drawn from it (`assets/brand/`).** Four SVGs — the mark, the app tile, a maskable tile and a graphite one for admin — plus `pnpm build:icons`, which copies the SVG favicons and renders the PNG sizes into each app's `public/`. Nothing under `apps/*/public/` is hand-edited any more. *Why:* the icon all three apps shipped was the turbostack placeholder, a blue neural-network cloud, which is the wrong product in the wrong palette; and `apps/admin` had no favicon at all, so an admin tab and a candidate tab looked identical in a tab strip. The mark is two overlapping circles with the ground they share filled in — a match is what two people have in common — in the `--primary` wine and `--background` cream, at the lightest stroke that still holds together at 16px.
  - **SVG wherever a platform allows one, PNG only where it does not.** A favicon is `icon.svg`; the generated PNGs exist for the two PWA manifests (Android wants raster) and for `apple-touch-icon`, which iOS has never read as SVG. Both manifests now point at a real `icon-maskable-512x512.png` rather than handing Android the rounded tile and letting it crop the corners off.
  - **The SVGs are the only place the palette is hardcoded**, because a standalone SVG cannot read a CSS variable. If a token moves, those four files move with it — the README says so, next to them.
- **What the AI is costing, at `/admin/usage` (prd phase 2 §6, "cost control").** Every call that leaves this deployment for a model now records its tokens, and a new admin page adds them up by day, by agent, by model and by matchmaker over a window of 1, 7 or 30 days. *Why:* §9.2 has had one row reading "not built, and now the gap that matters most" — the per-matchmaker budget — and a budget is a number you cannot choose without first knowing what a message actually costs. This does not build the budget. It builds the only thing the budget can be set from.
  - **The gateway reports tokens and no price, so the two are kept apart everywhere.** `@convex-dev/agent` passes along the AI SDK's `usage` block and the Convex AI gateway is an OpenAI-compatible endpoint: there is exactly one fact per generation, which is how many tokens. Every figure in dollars is arithmetic over a rate somebody typed into the table at the bottom of the page. **An unpriced model has no cost, not a cost of zero** — a zero in a total is a claim that a bill was paid off — and the page says how many generations a total is missing rather than letting it read as the whole of it.
  - **A rate is a setting, not a constant**, for the reason an agent's model is one (§4.4): a price changes without our releasing anything, and a deploy is the wrong ceremony for correcting one. Both halves or neither, because an input rate with no output rate would bill every reply as free. Every change is audited as `ai_model_rate.updated` with the old numbers in the event, which is where a rate's history lives.
  - **A generation is priced when it happens, and stays priced.** Correcting a rate today changes what the next run costs and cannot rewrite what last month is reported to have cost. The alternative — pricing at read time — would make a page whose figures changed under the reader.
  - **Recording can't cost a matchmaker their drafts.** `recordUsage` never throws: every caller is an action in the middle of something somebody is waiting on, and it is written *before* the model's text is even parsed, because the tokens were spent whether or not the parse found anything. A reconciler quietly burning tokens for no entries is exactly the run this page exists to show.
  - **Days are UTC, and the page says so.** A local-day bucket would make a row's day depend on where the reader was standing, and two admins in two time zones would disagree about yesterday.
  - **One read with a visible ceiling**, rather than a rollup table. At phase-2 volumes a month is a few hundred rows, and a daily rollup would be a second copy of this data to keep true — so the window is one index range read newest-first, and a window that exceeds the ceiling says so on the page instead of quietly reporting a smaller bill.
  - Seeded generations are a new kind of scenario row (`seed/e2e/scenario.ts`), because they are a state the suite cannot otherwise reach at all: the gateway needs a paid Convex Cloud deployment and the e2e backend is a local anonymous one, so no spec can make a real model call.
- **A profile that fills itself, and a voice the product learns (prd phase 2 §4.1B, §4.1C, build step 3).** The last two of the three agents now write. A candidate says something about themselves in the thread; a few seconds later it is on their profile, or waiting above the composer as a proposal, with their own words beside it so the matchmaker can check it. And every twentieth message a matchmaker sends, the voice agent reads what they have written and offers a description of how they write. *Why:* phase 2 built two doors into a profile and left them shut — the write paths, their policies and the cards that render a proposal have all been there for days with nothing coming through them.
  - **The noticing rides along with the drafting call.** The conversation agent has just read the new messages to draft from them, so it is asked, in the same generation, what those messages revealed — `REPLIES` first, because that is the half a matchmaker sees, then `NOTICED`, one line per observation with the candidate's exact words. A second call over the same text would have paid twice for the same reading. **A model that ignores the headings has still written drafts:** the whole text falls through to the replies parser and nothing is noticed, which is the feature exactly as it shipped last week.
  - **An observation with no quote is dropped.** The quote is what a matchmaker checks a proposal against, and an observation nobody can trace back to a message is the confident invention this pipeline exists to keep out of a profile.
  - **The profile agent gets its own thread, not the drafting one.** A drafting thread's history is the context the next reply is written in, and a turn spent arguing about registry keys is not something a reply should be written in the shadow of. Both threads are forgotten together — by the per-conversation switch, and by an erasure.
  - **It is never asked whether to write or to propose.** That is the field's policy, applied by `applyAgentEntries`; an agent that could choose would make the policy advisory. The prompt does not contain the words *suggest* or *propose*, and a test asserts it does not. The registry goes into the prompt minus the fields reserved for the matchmaker — listing a field only to forbid it invites a model to reach for it, and the value would be refused downstream anyway.
  - **The voice agent runs on a count, not a clock, and keeps no thread.** Every 20 sent messages (`AI_VOICE_SAMPLE_MESSAGES`), reading twice that many so a voice does not swing with whatever kind of week they have just had, and only messages they typed — an imported history is partly the candidate's words. The mark moves when a run is *scheduled*, so two messages either side of the threshold cannot buy two generations over one sample.
  - **No UI changed.** The suggestion stack above the composer, the Profile section and the settings page were all already reading these doors; they had nothing to render outside the dev seed and now they do.
- **An erasure reaches the model's copy of a person (prd phase 2 §4).** `eraseAccount` deletes the agent threads on every conversation it anonymises, through the component's own API, and the admin page reports how many. *Why:* §4 admitted `@convex-dev/agent` on exactly one condition — that an erasure could reach its tables, which `ctx.db` cannot see — and the reply suggester crossed that line three days ago without anyone noticing. An erasure was anonymising the person everywhere the database could see and leaving a verbatim thread standing where it could not.
  - **The door the PRD named was the wrong one.** `users.deleteAllForUserId` keys on a component user id, and these threads carry none — they are per conversation, not per person. Giving them one would have worked for every thread created after the change and silently missed every one before it. Walking the conversations is exact and needs no backfill.
  - The test seeds **real threads in the component's own tables** and proves they are gone afterwards. Verified the way a regression test is worth verifying: with the fix removed it fails, with it restored it passes.

### Fixed
- **A rendered clip was longer than its timeline, by its own closing hold.** `render-demo.mjs` lists the final still twice so the last frame holds instead of flashing past — but the repeat *inherits* the duration it was given, so a `hold(4500)` closed the clip on nine seconds of one frame and every mp4 came out exactly `manifest total + final hold` long. The hold is now split across the two entries. *Why it matters:* the whole premise of capturing frames plus a timeline is that the numbers in the capture are the edit, which is not true if the renderer silently doubles one of them.
- **`Director.type` threw on `<input type="email">`** (`InvalidStateError` from `setSelectionRange`). HTML only defines the selection APIs for `text`, `search`, `url`, `tel` and `password`; the caret is now placed only where that is defined, and skipped where it is not — a field with no selection API holds no draft, so the click that focused it has already left the caret in the right place.
- **The panel's edit affordance was hover-only, which on a phone means never (`apps/app/src/workspace/panel-record.tsx`).** A row you can change showed its pencil under the pointer and nowhere else, so on a touch screen the whole record read as flat text — and this app is heading for a phone. The pencil is now always drawn, faint, firming up on hover and focus; the same goes for a note's Edit and Remove. A column of faint pencils down the right margin reads as an editable gutter rather than as noise, and a row nothing can change here still offers none — which is the distinction worth drawing.
- **The Status value sat a few pixels left of every other value (`apps/app/src/workspace/candidate-panel.tsx`).** It is a bare `<select>`, and a native select insets its own text by an amount that belongs to the browser rather than to us; no padding we set takes it back, and the offset differs per browser, so the negative margin that squared it up on one platform was wrong on the next. `appearance-none` drops the inset with the rest of the native chrome, and the chevron is drawn by hand — the one the accordion headers already use. Two specs now hold both of these: that an unhovered row still offers its pencil, and that the status control's text starts where the value above it does.

### Changed
- **A profile stores a date of birth, not an age (`packages/api/convex/candidateProfiles/rules.ts`).** The registry carried both: `dateOfBirth`, which the agent could only propose, and `age`, which it could write straight in. The `age` field is gone. *Why:* an age is true for one year and nothing stored beside it knows which year that was, so a profile written last spring quietly matches on a number that is now wrong — and the record it sits in is meant to outlive the conversation it came from. A birth date is true for good.
  - **An age a candidate mentions goes to `notes.age`**, in prose, with when they said it — a new suggested note. The registry's reason for making a birth date a proposal rather than a write has not changed: a model that reads "I'm 34" and writes a birth date has invented eleven months of it. So it is told, in as many words, to write the age it heard and never to work a birth date back from one.
  - **Matching reads the birth date and only the birth date.** `ageOf` (`matches/rules.ts`) no longer falls back to the `age` fact, so a pair with no birth date between them scores nothing on the age signal and passes the `partnerAgeRange` filter rather than being judged on a stale number. A spec states that cost out loud rather than leaving it to be discovered.
  - **Any `age` fact already stored is inert**, not migrated: it is no longer a registry key, so nothing reads it and nothing can write it. The dev seed carries birth dates now.
- **Both agents are told what day it is (`packages/api/convex/ai/rules.ts`, `todayLine`).** Neither had a clock. "My birthday's tomorrow" was a date no model could resolve, "I'm 34" arrived undated, and a drafted reply could not say "see you Thursday" and mean it. Every turn now opens with the weekday and the ISO date. *Why:* this mattered less while an age was a number the agent could write down; with a birth date as the only age on the record, a relative date is the whole path from what someone says to what gets stored.
  - **It goes in the per-turn instruction, never the opening brief.** A thread outlives the day it was opened — the profile agent is briefed once per conversation and then talked to for months — so a date stated in the brief is a date that quietly goes wrong.
  - UTC, and the line says so: a matchmaker in Auckland or Los Angeles is within a day of it, and claiming more precision than that would be a guess.
- **A note's name is typed, not spelled (`apps/app/src/workspace/candidate-profile.tsx`).** "Something else…" asked for a key — the placeholder read `idealWeekend`, and a space was a validation error. The field now takes free text and shows back the key it will store when you leave it: "Ideal Weekend" becomes `ideal_weekend`, which the row renders as "Ideal weekend". `slugifyNoteKey` runs on blur rather than on every keystroke, because rewriting what someone is halfway through typing moves the caret out from under them, and again on submit for anyone who presses Add without leaving the field. Underscores join camelCase in `noteKeyError`, so every key already stored — and every key in the suggested list — is still valid.
- **The candidate panel's Matches card takes the whole column (`apps/app/src/workspace/candidate-matches.tsx`).** An arrow sat either side of it, costing the card about a seventh of the panel's width in gutters — in the narrowest column of the product — to say what the header above it was already there to say. The stage, the counter and both arrows are now one line above the card: `Introduced … ‹ 1/2 ›`, the arrangement the suggestion stack already uses, which is why `components/carousel.tsx` keeps its counter separate from its arrows. A spec asserts the card is as wide as the section it sits in, so the gutters cannot creep back.
- **The History trail is grouped by day (`apps/app/src/workspace/candidate-history.tsx`).** Every entry carried a full date *and* time — "Sep 21, 2026, 5:54 PM" under each of twenty lines, nineteen of them from the same afternoon. The day is now a heading over the run of events that share it, in the same small uppercase style Details and Profile use, and an entry keeps only who and what time. The `<time>` element still carries the whole ISO timestamp, so nothing machine-readable was dropped.
  - `groupByDay` (`workspace/history-days.ts`, with tests) gathers **consecutive runs** rather than bucketing by key: paging appends older events, and an out-of-order event should show where it sits rather than teleport under an earlier heading. "Today" is decided once per render against one clock, not per entry.
- **The candidate panel's Details section is a record too, on the same rows Profile uses (`apps/app/src/workspace/candidate-panel.tsx`).** It was a standing form — a name box, a stack of handle rows each with its own select, input and ✕, a **Save details** button and a status line — roughly 480px of controls for five things worth knowing, in the section a matchmaker lands on when they open a thread they haven't touched in a week. It is now one line each, and the four sections of the panel fit on screen together. *Why:* Details is read far more often than it is edited, and the form was charging everyone the edit's price.
  - **The rows are shared, not copied (`apps/app/src/workspace/panel-record.tsx`).** `RecordGroup` / `RecordRow` / `RecordEditRow` are what both sections render, so the next section added to the panel inherits the answer instead of inventing a third one.
  - **A row with no pencil is a row you cannot change here.** Email and membership read as plain lines; name and each handle are rows you click. Status keeps a `<select>`, stripped of its box so it reads as the row's value and keeping the native arrow that says it is a control — it is a closed list of three that takes effect on change, so there was never anything for a Save button to do.
  - **Each handle is its own row**, edited and removed in place, and adding one folds away behind a button. Every write still sends the name and the whole handle list, because that is what `updateDetails` takes — but each row reads the other half off the candidate rather than out of a draft it has held since mount, so an edit made elsewhere is no longer silently overwritten.
  - **Two `Field`s per handle form, not one round both controls.** Base UI's `Field` labels *the* control inside it, so a `Field` holding a select and an input points both at the same label: the select's own `aria-label` loses to the inherited `aria-labelledby`, and "Platform" and "Handle" each resolve to two elements. Caught by the specs, not by types.
  - Page objects follow: `getDetail(field)`, `getNameRow()`, `openName()`, `setName()`, `getHandle(platform)`, `openAddHandle()`, `addHandle()`. `getSaveDetailsButton()` and `getDetailsStatus()` are gone — the row closing on the new value is the confirmation, the same way it works in Profile.
- **The candidate panel's Profile section reads as a record, not a form (`apps/app/src/workspace/candidate-profile.tsx`).** A fact was three lines — label and value, then a permanent row carrying "You added this", **Edit** and **Clear** — so five facts filled the screen and the panel was mostly chrome. A fact is now **one line**: label left, value in a second column you can scan straight down, and the whole row is the edit control, with a pencil fading in on hover. Eight facts and their group headings now sit where five used to overflow. *Why:* this is the narrowest column in the product, and it is a reading surface — a matchmaker opens it to *know something*, and only sometimes to change it.
  - **Provenance is marked where it carries information.** "You added this" on every row of a record you keep is noise; a value a model touched is the exception, so that one gets a visible ✦ and the rest keep the full wording in the row's `title`, in a screen-reader line, and in the edit form — where you are about to replace the value is exactly where its source is worth reading.
  - **Clearing moved inside the edit form**, since the read row has no room for a second button and emptying a field is worth the extra click. The page object follows (`clearField` opens the row first).
  - **Add a field and Add a note fold away** behind a button, so the section no longer ends in a form that is empty nine visits out of ten. `openAddField()` / `openAddNote()` on the page object unfold them.
  - **The `sr-only` line cost a re-run and is worth writing down:** `sr-only` is `position: absolute`, and an absolutely positioned box whose ancestors are all unpositioned is **not clipped by their `overflow`** — the one in each row sat at its static offset in the *document* and gave the window 1163px to scroll, which `specs/app-convex/layout.spec.ts` caught. The rows carry `relative` for that reason alone.
  - Notes keep their cards; their Edit and Remove are icon buttons that appear on hover, and the group headings are small uppercase labels aligned with the values' left edge.
- **The conversation header's two controls now say what they are.** The per-conversation switch for drafted replies is a real switch, labelled **AI**, and **Details** is gone from `lg` up. *Why:* both were controls that didn't look like what they did — one a ghost button with a dot beside the word "Suggestions", the other a button that opened a panel already open beside it.
  - **`@repo/ui` gained `Switch`** (Base UI's `Switch`, root and thumb as one element), styled from its own `data-checked` rather than from a `className` the caller threads through. What it replaced was a ghost `Button` wearing `role="switch"` by hand, with a coloured dot for the state — the semantics of a switch without the affordance of one.
  - **The label is what names it.** Base UI renders the switch as a `<span role="switch">` beside a hidden input and finds the enclosing `<label>` to point `aria-labelledby` at, so the wrapping label is load-bearing rather than decoration. Inside it, "AI" is `aria-hidden` and "Drafted replies" is `sr-only`: the computed name is **Drafted replies**, and the role announces on or off by itself. The old control was named "Turn off drafted replies" — an instruction, which is the wrong half of the sentence for a control that already says which way it is set.
  - **Details is `lg:hidden`,** matching the panel's own Close button, which has been hidden at the same breakpoint for the same reason: from `lg` up the panel sits alongside and there is nothing to open.
- **The marketing site says what the product does now (`apps/www`).** The landing page described phase 1 and nothing else: AI was "coming next", and the match board was what came after *that*. Both have shipped, so for two build steps the site's most prominent claims about the roadmap were claims about work already done. Reply drafting and the board are now features rather than promises, and what is genuinely unbuilt is the only thing left in the roadmap callout.
  - **Four steps, not three.** *How it works* ended at "carry on in the chat", which was the whole product when it was written. The fourth step is the board (`prd/phase-3.md` §2), and it is what stops the page describing half the job — a matchmaker reading the old page learned that we store conversations, not that we score their book.
  - **Three feature cards added:** drafts in your voice (`prd/phase-2.md` §4A), the structured profile (§3), and the board with its arithmetic on the card. The board card says *no model picked it* in as many words: it is the unusual claim, and "AI matchmaking" is what a reader assumes otherwise.
  - **The hero still shows a drafted reply.** Its last outbound bubble is now the suggestion card it would be today — above the composer, never in the message list, with Send, Edit and Dismiss. Same height, so the hero did not grow; and the one shipped feature a still can actually show is now in it.
  - **The roadmap callout is the part that goes stale by standing still,** so it is now pinned by a test that asserts from both sides: shipped AI must be a feature card, unshipped AI must be in the callout. The old test asserted the reverse — that no feature card mentions suggested replies — and passed happily for two build steps after that stopped being true.
  - **Copy that had quietly become false:** the "It works without the AI" principle claimed the first version has no AI in it; the *Where does AI come in?* answer said "Next"; *What's coming next?* promised the board. Each now says what runs. A new FAQ answer covers how the matching works, because a deterministic scorer that shows its arithmetic is a selling point a reader will otherwise assume away. Privacy gains a line saying matching never leaves your own book.

### Fixed
- **The hero's product still overflowed a phone by 14px (`apps/www/components/landing/hero.tsx`).** Its column is a grid item, and a grid item's `min-width` defaults to `auto` — so the column was sized by the mock's min-content (~373px) instead of by its 343px track, and the card ran under the right-hand gutter. The section's `overflow-hidden` clipped it silently, which is why nothing caught it: no horizontal scrollbar, no failing assertion, just a card with its right edge shaved off on the one screen size the product is built for. `min-w-0` makes the track the constraint. Predates this change; found while checking the taller mock on a phone.

### Changed
- **`candidateName` moved from `replySuggestions/helpers.ts` to `candidates/helpers.ts`** as `candidateDisplayName`. It was private to the drafting run until the profile run needed the same answer, and "what do we call this person" belongs to the domain that owns the person rather than to the first agent that asked.
- **The PRD now says what got built (`prd/`).** Phase 2's reply suggester shipped as build step 2 and the file still described it as proposed — §1, §2, §4, §4.1A, §5, §6 and §8 all marked it as work not started, three days after it went out. Those sections now say what runs, and the README's status table stops calling phases 2 and 3 drafts when each is half built. *Why:* a status marker nobody maintains is worse than no marker, because the next reader plans against it.
  - **The one thing this found that is a defect rather than a stale sentence:** phase 2 §4 admitted `@convex-dev/agent` on one condition — its tables are invisible to `ctx.db`, so an erasure has to reach them through `components.agent.users.deleteAllForUserId`, and **nothing was to create a thread until that was wired**. The reply suggester creates one per conversation and `admin.mutations.eraseAccount` still doesn't knock on that door, so an erasure anonymises the person everywhere `ctx.db` can see and leaves the model's copy of what it was shown standing. Recorded in §4 and in `convex.config.ts` rather than quietly fixed, because the sentence that stopped being true was load-bearing. Wiring it comes before build step 3 ([#3](https://github.com/cloudexible-org/match.build/issues/3)).
  - **Three requirements were quietly wrong and are restated, not deleted.** §6 asked for a first token under ~2 s through `persistent-text-streaming`, written for a draft a matchmaker waits on; what shipped is triggered by the candidate's message and lands beside a composer they can already type in, so there is no first token to be under 2 s of — the requirement is now that a run never delays a message and fails silently. §9.1 named two Anthropic models that are not the ones seeded; models are a database setting on `/admin/ai`, so the PRD no longer names any. And §4's component table now carries the state of each: one installed, three not, with what each is still waiting for.
  - **§9.1 is headed "must be settled before any code", and prompt-injection wasn't.** Said so in the file, with what actually stands in its place today (a seeded instruction, a human pressing Send, one candidate per run) and why that isn't the mechanism the bullet asks for. It has to be settled before the extractor starts writing what a message told it to.
  - Phase 3's dependency on phase 2 is marked half-met: the board reads `candidateProfiles`, but nothing fills one automatically yet, so every fact the nightly scorer reads was typed by hand. Its §3 also notes that `/c/mm/discover` exists as a deliberate placeholder with nothing linking to it.

### Added
- **Demo clips of the working app, for the pitch deck (`tooling/marketing`).** `pnpm capture:demo` drives the app through a real feature and writes **timed frames plus a manifest**; `pnpm render:demo` turns those into a 1920×1080 mp4, a GIF and a poster. The first one, `inbox-ai-reply`, is ten seconds: the book, Sam's thread, a reply already drafted in the matchmaker's voice, edited and sent. *Why:* a deck needs the product moving, and a screen recording of a dev server is a different thing every time you make one.
  - **Frames and a timeline, not a recording.** Playwright records webm directly, but a recording's pacing is the *run's* pacing — every Convex round trip lands in the clip as dead air and the length changes run to run, so a ten-second demo cannot be trimmed out of it. The capture records what was on screen and how long each thing should be *on screen for*; `hold(1100)` is one still held for 1.1s however long it took to settle. The durations are the edit: change the numbers, re-render, no browser involved. Transitions are interpolated from the two endpoint stills by ffmpeg's `xfade`, so a dissolve is identical on every re-run and independent of the app's own router animation.
  - **The pointer is drawn into the page** (`lib/cursor.ts`). Screenshots contain no cursor, so a captured click would be indistinguishable from a jump cut. Every visual is an explicit setter, never a CSS animation — frames are shot seconds of wall clock apart, so a self-animating ripple would be sampled at a different phase in every take. The same init script freezes the app's transitions and the caret blink, so two shots of one state are byte-identical.
  - **Outputs land in `.scratch/`, never the repo.** Re-rendering a clip twenty times costs nothing and leaves no binaries in git history. Needs `ffmpeg` (`brew install ffmpeg`).
  - See [`docs/demo-videos.md`](demo-videos.md), including the gotchas that each cost a re-shoot: `networkidle` is not "settled", a click moves the caret, and whoever you seed *last* is at the top of the list.

### Changed
- **No summariser in v1 (prd phase 2 §2).** It was build step 5 and is now cut, along with `conversations.summary` / `summarisedThroughSeq` from the schema sketch, the "keeps the summary" line from the conversation agent's remit, and the summary block from §4.2's context assembly. *Why:* a derived artefact that has to be kept correct, kept fresh and kept honest is a large thing to build on a guess about how much older context is worth. It comes back when a real thread is long enough to show what is missing.
  - **The live window goes 20 → 50 messages** and is now the whole of what the agent ever sees: there is nothing behind it, so message 51 of a long thread is simply not read. The standing instruction says as much — *"You are shown the last stretch of the thread rather than all of it, so work from what you have and never refer to something you were not given"* — because a model that thinks it has the whole conversation will write as though it does.
  - The agent's description at `/admin/ai` becomes "Drafts replies in the matchmaker's voice, and notices facts."

- **The reply suggester (prd phase 2 §4A, build step 2).** A candidate writes; a few seconds later the matchmaker has one to three drafts waiting above the composer, in their own voice, each with **Send**, **Edit** and **Dismiss**. *Why:* this is the feature phase 2 exists for — the rest of it is the machinery that makes this one trustworthy.
  - **The agent is briefed once, then kept up to date.** A conversation gets one `@convex-dev/agent` thread, and the thread is the memory. The first turn carries everything: the matchmaker's voice, the candidate's whole profile, the recent messages. Every turn after it carries only what has changed — the new messages, a voice that has been rewritten, profile entries that have moved, each marked as replacing what it was told before. `conversations` keeps three high-water marks (`agentBriefedSeq`, `agentBriefedVoiceAt`, `agentBriefedProfileAt`) so the delta is computable; re-sending the world every message would pay twice for what the thread already holds, and bury the new message under text the model has read four times.
  - **It is shown everything the matchmaker knows, their own notes included.** The earlier guardrail said the agent must never *see* that; it now says it must never *repeat* it. A drafter working from half the file writes worse drafts, and a note like "would introduce to Jordan first" is exactly what makes a good next question — while writing it back to the candidate would be a betrayal of the person who wrote it. The control is that nothing reaches anyone until a human presses Send. The seeded standing instruction says all of this in those terms, and the test that pinned the old wording now pins the new.
  - **Everything the matchmaker touches is on the record; the drafts are rows.** `replySuggestions` persists each one, so they survive a reload and going stale is a state rather than a guess. Four of them: `ready`, `stale` (the thread moved, or they replied in their own words), `sent`, `dismissed`. Nothing is deleted — what was offered and passed over costs less to keep than to explain later.
  - **Sending one is sending a message.** It goes out under their name, in the ordinary thread, and the candidate cannot tell. `messages.source` gains `ai_suggestion`, which records how the message started and not who is answerable for it. An edit is still a suggestion: the draft is what got them there. Sending one retires the alternatives, because that is what they were.
  - **Edit does not answer the card.** It drops the text into the composer and leaves the draft on offer, so changing your mind gives you the original back — and it never writes over a half-typed message, which is the one thing it must not do.
  - **Debounced, not per keystroke.** A message schedules a run ~5s later and cancels the one already pending, so a burst of typing is one generation. The matchmaker's own reply schedules one too: what they wrote is the best evidence of how they write, and the agent should have it before the next message rather than after.
  - **Three deployment settings, none of which can take the feature down**: `AI_REPLY_DEBOUNCE_SECONDS`, `AI_REPLY_COUNT`, `AI_REPLY_LIVE_WINDOW`. Unset falls back, nonsense falls back, out of range clamps — a typo in an env var is not a reason for a matchmaker to lose their drafts (prd §9.2).
  - **Every way out is quiet.** Agent unconfigured, switched off, no model, no gateway, thread moved on, model refused or failed — all end with no drafts, which is the phase-1 app working exactly as it did. A failure to draft must never be a failure to send.
  - **Not streamed yet.** prd §4A asks for `persistent-text-streaming` and a sub-2s first token; this generates complete drafts and writes them as rows. The table and the cards do not move when streaming lands — it is a change to one action.
  - **Where it lands.** A `convex/replySuggestions/` domain rather than inside `messages/` as prd §8 sketched: it owns a table, a registry of what gets sent, and its own write path, which is what `CLAUDE.md` §8 means by a domain. `rules.ts` has no Convex imports, so every word that leaves this deployment is unit-testable and readable in one file.
  - **Tests:** 28 unit cases over the briefing and the deltas — what the opening brief carries, that an update carries only what moved, that a rewritten voice is re-stated, and the forgiving parse of what comes back; 17 convex-test cases over the debounce collapsing a burst, staleness on both sides, sending as written and edited, the alternatives retiring, tenancy from three directions, and a run with no agent doing nothing quietly; and 7 Playwright cases driving Send, Edit and Dismiss against seeded drafts. **The generation itself has no e2e coverage and cannot have any:** the gateway needs a paid Convex Cloud deployment and the suite runs on a local anonymous one, which is also why `scenario.replyDrafts` exists.

- **The match board, and a deterministic matching algorithm behind it (prd phase 3 §2, §4, §5).** A new page at `/app/mm/:username/matches`: three columns — Proposed → Introduced → Connected — and a match that has ended leaves the board. A nightly cron scores every pair in every book and puts what it finds in Proposed. *Why:* phase 1 and 2 gave a matchmaker somewhere to keep what they know about people; this is the first thing the product does *with* it.
  - **A card is marked seen by a button, not by being read.** Unseen cards sort first, so marking on read reordered a card out from under the cursor of whoever was reading it — which teaches people not to open one. *Mark seen* is now an explicit control on the card, beside *All N reasons*. Moving or closing a card still counts as having seen it, because both of those are decisions rather than a glance.
  - **Three columns, because each one is something that happened between two people.** The first cut had five, and two of them were describing the matchmaker rather than the match: *Reviewing* was a state of mind, and *Mutual interest* was a fact the next column already implies — you do not put two people in touch before they have both said yes. A card had to be dragged through both to say nothing anybody could act on. What *Reviewing* was really for — telling a card you have read from one you haven't — is a property of the card, so it is a dot on the card and unseen cards sort first.
  - **One way for a match to end, not two.** A rejection and a wedding are the same event: somebody saying this is over and saying what happened. So the Rejected lane is gone, and there is one `closed` state carrying an outcome — *They're together* or *It didn't work* — plus who ended it, where somebody did (you, either of them, or **both of them**, which is a different fact from either one and the commonest way an introduction quietly ends), and a note. A closed match leaves the three live columns for a **Closed column of its own, hidden until it is asked for** — and the way in is the count, **"14 closed · 3 together"**, sitting above the board with the other controls rather than under it. What is over is worth keeping and worth counting, and it is not work; a count in the corner reads as a way in, where a permanent fourth column reads as a fourth thing to do. Nothing ages out any more, because nothing needs hiding. Closed is deliberately **not** a drop target: closing takes what happened, and a column that lit up under a dragged card and then refused it would be a column that lied. Closing as together offers to archive both of them, through the same status a matchmaker sets by hand, since two people who found each other are not in the book to be matched with anyone else.
  - **The per-candidate yes/no is gone with the column it fed.** A no is not a sub-state, it is the end of the match, and it is recorded where it belongs — on the closing record, with whose no it was. A yes is implied by the matchmaker doing the next thing.
  - **No AI, on purpose.** The PRD's pipeline is "hard filter on facts → AI compatibility reasoning"; this is the first half, and for now it is the whole of it. Every card is produced by `convex/matches/rules.ts` — plain code, no Convex imports, exported through `@repo/api` so the board renders exactly the reasoning the run recorded. A matchmaker can be told precisely why two people are on a card together, and the same book scored twice gives the same answer twice.
  - **Missing data never disqualifies.** A blank field is something nobody has recorded yet, not a "no". Every hard filter needs *both* halves of itself on the record before it fires, and each is mutual: gender against what each of them is looking for, age against each stated range, height, the registry's own word `dealbreaker` for children, "a partner having children is fine", and a required shared religion. A partner who merely *should* want children is a preference, so it scores badly and blocks nothing.
  - **Every score is symmetric, because a pair is unordered.** The two ids are stored sorted and `pairKey` is the pair itself, unique across the table — which is what stops tonight's run suggesting the same two people last night's did, and makes "are they already on the board?" one indexed read. One-sided preferences are evaluated both ways and averaged.
  - **A score is only as good as its coverage.** Two near-empty profiles agree about everything: they score 100 and still don't reach the board, because the score is the weighted mean over the signals that *could* be read and `coverage` says how much of the possible weight that was. A card needs 50 and 35% coverage, both named constants in one file. The board shows them together — "82 — on a full profile" — so a number is never offered on its own.
  - **A card leads with why, including why not.** The run stores the signals it read, and the card shows the strongest few *plus the weakest one when it is genuinely weak*: "Both want marriage · Both in Toronto · Smoking: never and regularly". A card that listed only what agrees would lie by omission, and the one axis where two promising people don't line up is the thing a matchmaker most needs to see. Free-text `dealbreakers` are flagged rather than read — no algorithm here understands them, and the card says so instead of implying they were checked.
  - **The nightly run never reopens a decision a person made.** It only revises its own untouched `proposed` cards: a rescoring is patched quietly (the profile change behind it is already in the trail, and a nightly event per card per book would bury the events that matter), while a card it *withdraws* — the pair has become impossible, or fallen below the bar — is audited, closed, and marked `closedBy: "system"`, the one ending nobody chose. Running it twice in a row changes nothing the second time.
  - **The run keeps the board in step with the book, not only with the profiles.** A suggestion whose pair it never looked at — somebody left, was archived, or had their profile emptied — is taken back with "one of them is no longer in your book", keeping the score it had while they were both in it. A board should not be offering an introduction that can no longer be made.
  - **One transaction per book, fanned out by the cron.** A single transaction covering every tenant would be one read limit away from a night where nobody gets any matches, and one slow book away from delaying the rest. Twenty new cards per book per run, so a matchmaker opens a shortlist rather than a list; the rest wait for tomorrow and the run says how many it held back.
  - **The same pass runs on demand.** "Find matches" runs it now, as the matchmaker, and reports what it looked at as well as what it found — "Looked at 12 profiles, 66 pairs · 4 new suggestions". An algorithm that only speaks at 3am can't be learnt from, and "nothing new" means something completely different over sixty pairs than over one.
  - **Everything a card can do is a command as well as a drag.** Cards are `draggable` and columns are drop targets, but every move is also in the card's menu — not a fallback, but the same command in a second place, because a board that could only be driven by dragging is one half the people who need it can't use. Closing is reachable only through a form that takes what happened; a note is required for a no, because that reason is the taste signal the board exists to collect, and optional for a yes, because making somebody write a sentence about good news is how good news stops getting recorded. Moving a card back onto the board clears what closing recorded, because a match that is back on the board is not one that ended.
  - **The board is the page.** It fills the workspace's frame and scrolls inside itself the way the chat shell does, rather than being a document that grows: the columns share the width when there is enough of it and stop at a readable 18rem when there isn't, each scrolls its own cards, and the board's one horizontal scrollbar sits at the foot of the window instead of wherever the tallest column happened to end. **Nothing in it sets a floor** — an early version put a minimum height on the board, and on a short window that made the page scroll past the bottom of it into nothing at all, because the columns had already given up their slack. A page scrollbar on a board should lead to a column or not exist; here it doesn't exist, and a spec measures that at four window heights.
  - **And the card is `relative`, which is the other half of that.** A card carries an `sr-only` label for the new-card dot, and an absolutely positioned element with no positioned ancestor has the page itself as its containing block — so *no* scroll container clips it. A column of fifteen cards was therefore stretching the **document** to the height of its content while every element in the chain, body included, stayed exactly the height of the window: the frame did not scroll, and the page did, into nothing. The spec now asserts the document cannot scroll either, and fails without the fix.
  - **Nothing reaches a candidate.** The introduction moment is still undesigned (§6): *Introduced* means the matchmaker showed each of them the other, and *Connected* means they put the two in touch. Both are things the matchmaker did and then recorded; the app sends neither of them anything.
  - **Manual pairing produces an identical card**, `origin: "manual"`, scored by the same algorithm — *including* a pair the hard filters refuse, which is the point of having it: a matchmaker who has met both of them knows something the profiles don't. The card still says what the algorithm thought, and the audit event says the pair was made against it.
  - **Every stage change is audited on both candidates' trails**, as two events, with `relatedEntityId` pointing at the other person so a reader can follow the card back. A trail is read one person at a time, and an event filed under only one of them would leave the other's history missing the introduction they were half of. Four audit actions, a `matches` entity table, and a **Matches** filter on the History tab — which also meant teaching `audit/rules.ts` to render a stored `didnt_work` as the words the board uses.
  - **Deviations from the phase 3 draft, all deliberate:** the five columns and the Rejected lane become three columns and a closed state (above); `origin` is `algorithm` rather than `ai`; `reasoning` (AI prose) becomes `signals`, the arithmetic the run actually did; the two response fields become one closing record; and `pairKey` answers the draft's open question about one match per unordered pair. The draft's question about the lane's ageing period is answered by there being no lane.
  - **`pnpm --filter @repo/api seed:dev:reset` empties the board**, so the next seed builds a fresh one. The seed is additive everywhere else — it adds what is missing and changes nothing that is there — and the board is the one thing that cannot work that way, because it is only built when it is empty and a seed that reshuffled cards would undo an afternoon of moving them. It deletes rows, which nothing else in the product does, and is allowed to because it refuses to run anywhere but a dev deployment. The audit events the old cards wrote are left alone: the trail is append-only, and a dev trail carrying a few stale match events is a smaller lie than one this had edited.
  - **The dev seed has a book worth matching: sixteen people with filled-in profiles, and a board of twenty-one cards.** Between them a couple of pairs the algorithm is confident about, thin ones, pairs the filters refuse outright, two men looking for men, one woman not looking for a single gender, and somebody nobody in the book is looking for. The seed runs the *real* pass — a seeded board the nightly run could never have produced is one you can't learn anything from — and then moves cards into **every state a card can be in**: two introduced, one connected, one closed as together, one turned down by a candidate, one where neither of them wanted it, some never looked at and one read and left. It also pairs two people **by hand** before the run, so the manual badge is on the board and the run leaves that pair alone.
  - **The brand gives way in the header inside a workspace.** One more nav link was one too many at 390px; the workspace's own nav is where you are and where you can go, and the name of the product is neither.
  - **Tests:** 61 unit and convex-test cases for the algorithm and the mutations — symmetry, unknown never disqualifying, each hard filter, coverage refusing a thin pair, idempotence, the run leaving touched cards alone, a withdrawal, tenant isolation, the fan-out — plus 20 for how a card reads, and nine Playwright cases in `app-convex/matches.spec.ts` driving the board end to end against a real backend. The scenario builder can now seed cards at any stage, since the run itself can only ever produce `suggested`.
- **The dev seed writes profiles, so the suggestion cards have something to show.** `seed/dev` predated `candidateProfiles` and wrote none at all, which meant a local `pnpm dev` could not display the stack above the composer at all — the one surface with no way to reach its own state by clicking, because only an agent opens a proposal and none runs yet. *Why:* a feature you cannot see locally is a feature nobody will notice is broken.
  - **Sam Candidate carries four open proposals**, one of each way a card can read: a value where nothing was recorded (`locationCity`), one that replaces what is there (`lookingFor`, `unsure → long-term`), one asking for an entry to go (`pets`), and a free-text note. So the row has arrows and a `1/4` counter. **Jordan Member carries one**, so the same row appears without either — a different card, worth seeing beside it. **Alex Member carries none.**
  - **Every quote is something the candidate actually says in the seeded thread**, so the verbatim line under a proposal is checkable rather than lorem. `sourceQuote` is now seedable in the e2e scenario builder too (`quote`), and `chat-suggestions.spec.ts` asserts it renders — it was reachable only from an agent before, which is to say from nowhere.
  - **Maya's own voice gets a draft waiting on it**, so the "Suggested change to your profile" row is present in *every* conversation until it is answered. That is the correct behaviour rather than a seeding accident: it is about the matchmaker, not whoever they happen to be reading.
  - **Additive one entry at a time, not one row at a time.** These people are seeded long before anyone clicks on them, so by the time a proposal is added to the fixture they already have a profile — and both a `continue` on the candidate and a guard on the profile row would have meant no proposal was ever written again. An entry already on the record keeps its value, its source and its timestamp, and only gains a `pending` if it has none; an entry that is not there is created; a proposal already open is left alone. Nothing is overwritten, which is also why a proposal you have answered comes back on the next seed: from the seed's side that is indistinguishable from one that was never written. The same rule covers the matchmaker's voice. The candidate, conversation and message writes moved into `seedMember` to make the split legible.

- **Suggestion cards above the message composer (prd phase 2 §5).** What the assistant is asking the matchmaker now sits at the bottom of the conversation, between the thread and the input: **one row per kind, and at most one card per row**. Four kinds are named and ordered in `apps/app/src/chat/suggestions.ts` — a suggested match, a change to the matchmaker's own profile, a change to the candidate's, and a drafted reply, the last of these nearest the box being typed in. *Why:* a proposal about someone is worth answering where the conversation that produced it is, and the candidate panel is behind a toggle on anything narrower than `lg`.
  - **A row is a carousel, not a pile.** The newest card shows; arrows either side step through the rest and stop at the ends rather than wrapping, with a `2/5` counter so the position is legible without counting. A row holding one card has neither. Left and Right do the same from the keyboard for anyone whose focus is inside the row, which is a `fieldset` for its native `group` role rather than a `div` wearing one.
  - **Dismiss answers the card showing and only that one.** The selection moves *before* the mutation runs: the query drops the answered card on its own, and a row that waited would fall back to the newest and move a card out from under whoever had arrowed to it. Answering the last card of a row steps back rather than emptying the screen.
  - **Two of the four rows have a source today**, and both of them already existed: the open `pending` proposals on this candidate's `candidateProfiles` entries, and the one open on the matchmaker's `voice`. Accepting or dismissing goes through `resolveSuggestion` / `resolveVoiceSuggestion`, so it is audited exactly as it is from the panel. The reply suggester (prd §4A) and the matcher (phase 3) are rows the stack already orders and nothing yet fills — when either lands it is one more `push` in `chat/conversation-suggestions.tsx` and no change to the stack.
  - **The same proposal is answerable in two places**, which prd §5 asks for: the panel's Profile section and this stack. Either one resolves it and the other stops showing it. How a change *reads* — `no → yes`, `Remove this — A cat`, the verbatim quote under it — was lifted into `workspace/profile-proposal.tsx` so the two surfaces cannot drift apart.
  - **Presentation is separate from data.** `chat/suggestion-stack.tsx` takes cards and knows nothing about Convex; `chat/suggestions.ts` is the grouping and selection logic with no React, unit-tested (14 cases) rather than driven through a browser. Six Playwright cases in `app-convex/chat-suggestions.spec.ts` cover the row-per-kind layout, the arrows stopping at both ends, a dismissal leaving its neighbours alone and surviving a reload, an acceptance reaching the profile, and the voice draft answered from the conversation.

- **The notifications panel is real: it reads the backend (`notifications.queries.feed`).** The bell shipped a week ago on a fixed sample; that sample is gone, and `useNotifications` is now a query and one mutation. What it shows: conversations with messages past your read marker, candidates who joined or left in the last month, and invitations waiting for your address — merged newest-first, capped at 20.
  - **Derived on every read, not stored.** There is no feed table and nothing writes a feed row: the panel is a projection of `conversations`, `candidates` and their read markers, so it cannot disagree with the workspace beside it. A stored "you have unread messages" is a copy that can be wrong, and the one thing worse than a missing notification is one for a message that isn't there. It costs a handful of bounded indexed reads, all through existing indexes but one — `candidates.by_matchmakerId_and_membershipChangedAt`, so recent joiners don't mean reading every candidate a matchmaker has.
  - **One item per conversation, and never the message.** Five messages from one person are one line that says "5 new messages". §8's rule — who wrote, never what they wrote — was written for lock screens and inbox previews, and it holds here too; a convex-test and an e2e spec both assert the body never reaches the panel.
  - **Opening the bell is not reading the message.** "Read" for the panel is one watermark per account, `notificationSettings.feedSeenAt`, set by `markFeedSeen` when the panel opens. The conversation's own marker still only moves when the thread is open (§8.1), so glancing at the bell quiets the bell and nothing else: the workspace keeps its unread count, and a push or email already scheduled still fires. This is the question the UI left open, answered — the badge means "new since you last looked", not "unread messages".
  - **A third channel §8 didn't name.** It has push's and email's rules but neither of their costs, so it deliberately skips their machinery: no delay, no coalescing table, no throttle. A membership change stops being news after a month, because nothing ever "reads" an acceptance and a quiet workspace's panel would otherwise be a list of who joined it last year. An invitation has no window — it is waiting on an answer for as long as it is open, and it expires on its own.
  - **Both sides of the product, one account.** A matchmaker who is also somebody else's candidate gets all three sources merged; a candidate who left is told nothing, the same rule the send path applies.
  - **The row is a real link again.** It was Base UI's `Popover.Close` rendering one, which stamps button semantics onto whatever it renders — the console said so under test. The panel is now controlled and closes itself on navigation, so the row is a plain `<Link>` that opens in a new tab like any other. `PopoverClose` is gone from the UI kit rather than left as a trap.
  - **The PRD now names three channels, not two.** §8 was written for push and email; the panel is neither, so it had no rules to inherit and none of its decisions were written down anywhere but here. New §8.3 says what it is derived from, why seen is not read, and why it has no switch, no delay and no throttle; sign-in moves to §8.4.
  - **Tests:** 16 convex-test cases for the query — both sides, coalescing, the private note that notifies nobody, reading clearing an item, the watermark being per account, signed out, and one account never seeing another's — plus rules tests for the ordering, the cap and the window. `specs/app-convex/notifications-panel.spec.ts` drives the loop end to end: someone writes, the bell counts it, opening quiets the bell while the workspace still says the message is waiting, and following the item lands on the thread.

- **`candidateProfiles` and `matchmakerProfiles`, replacing `notes` (prd phase 2 §3).** The matchmaker's record of a person becomes a structured document: `facts` keyed by a registry of ~48 typed fields, and `notes` keyed by whatever anyone names. The matchmaker's own profile gets the same treatment with one field so far — their `voice` — edited at `/app/mm/:username/settings`. *Why:* phase 2 needs somewhere for an agent to put what it learns, and "who is allowed to change this" has to be a property of the data rather than a convention each caller remembers.
  - **One document per candidate, not one row per fact.** This replaces the `facts` table the PRD had specified. Every value carries who wrote it, when, from which message, and whether a proposal is waiting on it — forty typed columns would have been forty nested objects, and adding a field would have been a schema migration every time. The registry in `convex/candidateProfiles/rules.ts` holds the types instead, so adding a field is an edit to one file. The cost is that a stored value is a string; it is a *normalised* one, because `valueError` then `normaliseValue` is the only way a value reaches the database, from a matchmaker's form and from an agent alike.
  - **The history of a value is the audit trail**, which was being written anyway. No supersession chain, no `status` column: the trail is already append-only and already refuses to be rewritten, and a second copy of that history would be less trustworthy than the one that doesn't. Which entry moved is `changes[].field` — `facts.wantsKids`, `notes.idealWeekend` — rendered through the registry so the History tab says "Wants children" and not a key.
  - **Three ways a value gets written, declared per field in the registry** (`matchmaker` / `agent` / `suggest`), not per matchmaker and not by a confidence threshold. `matchmaker` is the matchmaker's own judgement (`incomeBand`, `matchmakerNotes`); `suggest` is the special categories and anything a wrong guess costs a real person (`orientation`, `religion`, `politics`, `ethnicity`, `drugs`, `dateOfBirth`, and `voice`). A note key nobody has named defaults to `suggest`.
  - **This replaces the auto-apply confidence threshold in prd §9.2.** A model's confidence is not calibrated across fields: a threshold would have let a bad 0.9 on `orientation` through while blocking a good 0.7 on `pets`. `confidence` is still stored, to show and to tune against.
  - **An agent never overwrites what a person typed**, whatever the policy says — an `agent` field whose current source is `matchmaker` degrades to a proposal. One rule, enforced in one function (`agentWriteMode`), for a candidate's birth date and a matchmaker's voice alike.
  - **A proposal sits beside the value, never instead of it**, and there is at most one open per field. A later run that has learnt something better replaces it rather than queuing behind it, and the superseded one is audited rather than vanishing. A proposal can be a **removal** as well as a value, because an agent learns that something has stopped being true as often as it learns what is — and `value: ""` could not say so, since an empty value already means "nothing recorded". Writing a value does **not** answer an open proposal (whoever wrote it may never have seen it); writing exactly what it proposed, or removing an entry it proposed removing, does.
  - **Approving makes the value `agent_approved`, not `matchmaker`.** They agreed with it, they did not write it — so a later agent run may revise its own work, and still never theirs.
  - **Three directories, not two.** `convex/candidateProfiles/` and `convex/matchmakerProfiles/` each own a table and its functions; `convex/profiles/` registers no functions at all and holds the entry type, the validation and the write engine. One more directory than `CLAUDE.md` §8's one-per-domain, and the honest shape: the rule about who may write what has to mean the same thing in both.
  - **The agent's door is built and nobody has come through it.** `applyAgentEntries` and `applyAgentVoice` are internal mutations with no caller; the extraction that will feed them is prd §8 step 3. The caller says what it noticed and does *not* say whether to write or to suggest — an agent that could choose would make the policy advisory.
  - **A fourth writer is deliberately absent rather than stubbed:** a candidate editing their own profile (prd §9.3) would be one more `ProfileWriter` and one more policy in the registry, and nothing else would move.
  - **Erasure reaches it.** `anonymiseCandidateProfile` replaces the identifying and special-category facts the registry marks `personal` — birth date, city, occupation, orientation, religion — and leaves the rest standing, exactly as `status` and `membership` survive in the trail. Free-text notes are **not** redacted, on the same grounds phase 1 left note and message bodies alone: they are the matchmaker's own words, and whether a request reaches into them is the controller's call.
  - **UI:** the candidate panel's Notes section becomes **Profile**, rendering what is filled in rather than forty-odd empty rows, grouped by the registry, each value with its source and its verbatim quote, and an inline editor whose control comes from the field's value kind. Suggestions sit above the record and look unlike it. The History tab's Notes filter becomes **Profile**, and still covers the `note.*` events the old table left behind.
  - **A new `agent` variant of `auditActor`**, carrying the model it ran on — "the assistant changed this" is only half an answer once the model behind an agent has moved on.
  - **Tests:** 62 new unit and convex-test cases covering the policy engine, every value kind's validation and normalisation, the registry's own consistency (no duplicate keys, every group renders, every `choice` option is a value the field would accept back, the special categories are all `suggest`), all four write outcomes, a bad generation being dropped while the rest of its batch lands, a suggestion superseding a suggestion, every way a proposal can be resolved, removals under each policy, and the erasure. Two Playwright specs — `app-convex/candidate-profile.spec.ts` and the voice arc in `matchmaker-profile.spec.ts` — drive the whole thing through the UI, including a proposed removal.
  - **`notes` is gone, in one change rather than three.** A one-off internal mutation folded the surviving rows into a `notes.matchmakerNotes` entry, oldest first, and deleted them; removed notes stayed removed, because the History tab already records that they were removed. Prod held no rows and dev's two were migrated before the table came out of `schema.ts`, so the usual widen → migrate → narrow collapsed into a single commit — which is only safe because there was no third deployment to be out of step. The `note.created` / `note.edited` / `note.removed` audit actions stay in the vocabulary, and under the new **Profile** filter: the trail is append-only, so events written before the change still have to read.

- **A notifications overlay under the bell in the header — the UI only, on a fixed sample.** A panel anchored to the bell rather than a side drawer: a notification is read in passing and then acted on or ignored, so it should sit over the page for a second and leave. A drawer asks the page to move aside, which is the right gesture for something you work *inside* (the candidate panel) and the wrong one for a list you glance at. Full-width-minus-a-gutter at 375px, capped at 22rem and 60vh above that.
  - **`NotificationsMenu` (`apps/app/src/notifications/notifications-menu.tsx`) is a pure view.** It takes a list and one callback, `onOpen`, and owns nothing: what a notification *is*, when it becomes read and where it goes are the caller's. A row with an `href` is a link that closes the panel on the way out; one without is plain text rather than a control that looks clickable and isn't.
  - **Opening it is what marks everything read**, so there is no "mark all read" button — looking is the only thing that button ever meant. What the panel *shows* deliberately doesn't follow: the dots are snapshotted as it opens and held while it is on screen, because otherwise every dot vanishes under the cursor of the person who opened it to see which ones were new. The bell goes quiet immediately, which is the honest answer to "is there anything I haven't seen".
  - **`useNotifications` is the seam, and today it lies.** It returns four fixed items so the UI could be built and judged before there is anything to read. **Wiring means replacing that hook's body** — a query for the list and a mutation behind `markAllRead` — and deleting the sample; nothing above it changes. Until then the bell shows the same three unread to every signed-in person, so this must not ship to a real deployment as it stands.
  - **The badge stops counting at 9+, the label doesn't.** Three digits in a 1rem circle say nothing, but "Notifications, 12 unread" is exactly what a screen reader should hear — so the badge is `aria-hidden` and the count lives in the button's name. Unread is a dot *and* a heavier title, because weight alone isn't available to everyone.
  - **`Popover` joins the UI kit** (`packages/ui/src/components/popover.tsx`), beside `Menu`: a menu holds items you pick one of, a popover holds a panel with structure — a heading, a list, a button of its own. Portalled, with collision padding so a panel anchored top-right doesn't run off a phone.
  - **Tests:** unit tests for the unread count, the badge's ceiling, the unread snapshot outliving the read it describes, the short ages (`now`/`5m`/`3h`/`2d`/a date, with the year once it's another one) and initials. `apps/e2e/page-objects/app/notifications.page.ts` carries the selectors; there is no spec yet, because with a fixed list there is nothing a test could change.

- **AI agent settings come from the database and nowhere else (prd phase 2 §4.4).** There are no defaults in the code: `convex/ai/rules.ts` holds validation and labels and no prose at all, and nothing substitutes a model or an instruction at generation time. An agent nobody has configured is simply **off**. *Why:* a default in the code is a value that runs in production without anyone having chosen it, and the first person to read the module is the first to find out.
  - **A switch per agent**, plus **empty means off** for the model and the instruction. So there are four ways to be off — never set up, switched off, no model, no instruction — and the page says which one rather than just "off". `activeAgent()` is the single question a feature asks: it returns the settings or `null`, so no caller has to remember the four conditions.
  - **The starting values are seed data, not defaults** (`convex/seed/ai/fixture.ts`, applied by `ai:setup`). Idempotent — it never overwrites an admin's own edit — with `--force` to put an agent back. Unlike `seed/dev` and `seed/e2e` it wipes nothing and is safe against prod, because it is how a production deployment gets its agents at all.
  - **Seeded model is `openai/gpt-5.6-luna` for all three**, verified end to end through the gateway rather than assumed.
  - **The guardrails moved out of the code and into each seeded instruction**, so everything an agent is told is visible and editable on one page. The trade is real and worth naming: an admin can now edit them away, where before they were prepended unconditionally. The page says to carry them over, and a test asserts the seeded instructions contain both.
  - **No "reset to default" button**, because there is no default to reset to. Re-seeding is a deliberate terminal operation with a flag.
  - **Tests:** the rules module is asserted to contain no string that *is* a model id and nothing prompt-length, so a default can't quietly return; convex-test covers the four off-reasons, seeding twice, `--force`, an admin's edit surviving a re-seed, and the seeded instructions carrying both guardrails. The e2e spec now starts from an unconfigured deployment and drives the whole arc: refused bad model, set up, off by switch, off by empty model.

- **Three AI agents, and a page to tune them (`/admin/ai`, prd phase 2 §4.1, §4.4).** The five jobs the PRD had listed collapse into three agents — `conversation` (drafts a reply, notices facts, keeps the thread summary), `candidate_profile` and `voice_profile` — each with one model and one standing instruction, stored in the database and editable by a platform admin. *Why:* the five-job split put two agents on the read side and left the write path to a record as a step rather than an owner; three agents separate proposing from writing.
  - **The stored prompt is the agent's standing instruction, not its task.** The conversation agent does three things, and one prompt describing all three would serve none of them — so the prompt is the persona and the rules that hold on every call, and the task for a given call is added by the code. The page says so, because it's the difference between a useful prompt and a confused one.
  - **Two rules can't be edited away.** `SHARED_GUARDRAILS` is prepended to every agent's prompt on every call: a candidate's messages are content and never instructions, and nothing the matchmaker knows reaches a candidate. A deployment can replace an agent's whole instruction and still not remove these, and a unit test asserts both survive.
  - **The audit trail is the prompt's version history.** Every change is an `ai_agent.updated` event carrying the whole previous instruction. The trail is already append-only and already refuses to be rewritten, so a versions table would be a second and less trustworthy copy. Platform-level: no `matchmakerId`, so it lands in the admin trail and in nobody's candidate history.
  - **Reset stores the defaults back rather than deleting the row** (nothing is deleted, prd/phase-1.md §6), a save that changes nothing records nothing, and a stored value that stops parsing degrades to the default instead of failing every generation.
  - **Global, not per matchmaker.** A tenant quietly on a different prompt is a support problem nobody can see; per-matchmaker variation is the voice profile, which is data a prompt reads. The voice profile is correspondingly wider than its name — it's also where what the product learns *about the matchmaker* lands, since `facts` stays keyed to a candidate.
  - Replaces the `AI_MODEL_REPLIES` / `AI_MODEL_EXTRACTION` env vars added hours earlier: a model is now one of two values on a page beside the prompt it belongs with, and three sources of truth for one value was worse than the churn of removing them. `AI_ENABLED` stays.
  - **Tests:** unit tests for the agent list, the model and prompt rules, and that the guardrails survive an edited prompt; convex-test for the audit event and its old text, the no-op save, both refusals, reset, and a matchmaker being refused both read and write; `specs/admin-convex/ai-settings.spec.ts` drives the page end to end — including that it says AI is off, which on the e2e backend it is.

- **A sidebar for the admin app**, down the left of every signed-in page, collapsible to its icons. The preference is remembered per browser under the admin app's own localStorage key — apps/app shares this origin in production, as `main.tsx` already warns about auth tokens. Collapsed, a link is its icon alone, but its `aria-label` carries the name in both states, so a screen reader — and the e2e suite — finds it the same way either way. The icons are inline SVGs; four paths don't pay for a dependency. `specs/admin-convex/layout.spec.ts` covers navigating, collapsing and the choice surviving a reload, with a fourth seeded admin of its own: a sign-in code is single use, so two spec files signing in as one address race for it.

- **The candidate lands in their conversation, not on a chooser (prd §4.2).** An account with no matchmaker profile of its own now goes straight from `/` to `/app/c`, a three-column shell that mirrors the matchmaker workspace from the other side: their matchmakers on the left, the conversation in the middle, and who that matchmaker is on the right. *Why:* the home page asked every candidate to pick a side of a product they only have one side of, and the chat is the whole of what they came for.
  - **The matchmaker is in the hash** — `/app/c#maya.matches`, not `/app/c/maya.matches` — so switching between matchmakers swaps the thread without remounting the shell around it. The old path still works and redirects, because notification emails already sent name it; new notification emails link to the hash form (`notifications/rules.ts`), and `next=` through sign-in now carries the hash.

### Added
- **Erasure requests (`/admin/erasure`):** the resolution of the one place where "nothing is deleted" (prd §6) and a right-to-erasure request genuinely conflict. It erases the **person**, not the record. *Why:* a matchmaker's record of a relationship they ran is their own business record, and someone exercising their rights shouldn't take it with them.
  - **What goes:** the person's name, address and handles, everywhere they appear — their account, every matchmaker's candidate record, and the values inside the audit trail — plus copies of them that are nobody's record of anything (unsent emails in the outbox, a marketing sign-up). Their account is closed with it, so an erased person can't sign in and signing up again starts fresh.
  - **What stays:** every conversation, every message, every matchmaker's notes, and every audit event *as an event*. Each matchmaker keeps a complete history of what they did and when, attached to a candidate nobody can be identified from. A `candidate.anonymised` entry lands in each of their trails, because their book visibly changes and an unexplained change is worse than the news.
  - **The audit trail is redacted, not rewritten.** §5.3's append-only guarantee is narrowed rather than broken: an erasure replaces the personal *values* inside an event's `changes` and `reason` and never touches the event, its action, its actor or its time. Workflow values survive — "status: active → paused" still reads, "email: [erased] → [erased]" does not.
  - **One transaction with ceilings** (`ERASURE_LIMITS`). Past a limit it refuses and changes nothing; a half-erased person is worse than a refusal that says so. Running it twice is safe.
  - **Refused** for an account that owns a matchmaker profile — its candidates' conversations and notes hang off that profile — and without the account's own address typed back, checked on the server as well as in the page.
  - **Deliberately out of scope: message and note text.** That is free text, a matchmaker's notes are their own words, and the matchmaker is the data controller (prd §9.3) — how far a given request reaches is their call. The admin page says so; prd §12 keeps it open for legal review.
  - **Tests:** convex-test asserts the database holds no trace of the person afterwards (their name, both addresses, their handle and the free text they wrote), *and* that each matchmaker can still open the thread, the notes and the history through their own queries. `specs/admin-convex/erasure.spec.ts` drives it across both apps: the admin erases, and the matchmaker's open page shows an anonymous candidate with the conversation intact.

- **`doppler.yaml` still named the `matchmaker` project** after the rename, so the e2e suite couldn't start its app servers. Points at `matchbuild`, like `apps/e2e/doppler.ts` already did.

  - **Read markers decide everything.** Nothing is sent when a message is written; a job is scheduled, and when it fires it re-reads the conversation. If the recipient's marker has passed the message the row records `skipped_seen` and nothing goes out. "Seen" is the rule step 5 already implemented: the conversation open *and* the tab in front.

After an email is sent, no second email until that one has been read; push is capped at one a minute per conversation, and a message arriving inside the window is scheduled for the end of it rather than dropped.

Private messages and system notes notify nobody, and a candidate stops being notified the moment they are no longer a member.

The RFC publishes a complete worked example, and the test replays it byte for byte — which is the only way to know, since a push service accepts a badly encrypted request and the browser silently drops it. Subscriptions a push service reports as gone (404/410) are deleted.

The template's service worker is replaced by one that shows notifications and focuses the conversation they point at — and deliberately caches nothing, since an offline cache of a real-time chat shows stale conversations and a stale build.

- **Leaving and account deletion (phase 1 step 7):** the two ways a candidate's side of a relationship ends, and the read-only records that survive it. *Why:* a product that people can't leave isn't one you can honestly ask them to join, and the accept screen already promises what happens when they do.

Nobody has to explain themselves to leave. Their membership becomes `left` and every candidate-facing function stops answering for that conversation, because `requireCandidateSelf` requires `joined`.

Only its SHA-256 is stored, on the `users` row; asking again replaces it, five wrong guesses throw it away, and it can't be used to sign in — only `deleteAccount` accepts it. The token is re-derived in the action from the invite's nonce rather than passed in, so it never sits in the scheduler's stored arguments. A send scheduled for an invite that has since been replaced, answered or revoked does nothing. Links are `SITE_URL/invite/<token>`.
  - **The accept screen** at `/invite/:token` (the link works for any signed-in account; a signed-out visitor signs in and comes back) and at `/invitations/:candidateId` (home-page invitations, only for the verified invited address). It shows the matchmaker's name and the privacy notice, then Accept or Decline. Accept links the account, marks the candidate `joined`, uses up the link, and opens `/c/<username>`, which is a shell until chat lands in step 5. Used, revoked and expired links all say only "This invitation isn't valid any more".
  - **Refused, invite left open,** when the account owns the matchmaker profile, is already a member, or is linked to an older record in the same book (someone who left). The last case is a decision beyond the PRD: accepting would give one person two records with one matchmaker, so the matchmaker re-invites the old record instead.
  - **The matchmaker's banner** on an invited conversation now has **Resend** (3 emails a day per candidate, counting the first; counted from the audit trail, exact because mutations are serialisable), **Change email** (new token at the new address, so the old link dies; an address already in the book links to that candidate), and **Revoke**. Declined, departed and expired invites show when it happened and **Re-invite** (`membership.reinvited`). A candidate who accepted from a different address shows "Invited as … · Accepted as …".
  - **Expiry after 30 days** is a scheduled job per invite, which only clears the invite it was scheduled for. It hops at most 20 days at a time: JavaScript timers overflow past about 24.8 days, and convex-test runs the scheduler on them.
  - `AGENTS.md` §8 gains an `actions.ts` role for domains that call out of Convex (email now, LLMs in phase 2). `email/helpers.ts` has a generic `sendEmail`, and `emailOutbox.kind` gains `invite`.
  - **Tests:** convex-test for the whole lifecycle (email and link, accept by link and from the home page, the three refusals, expiry and the stale-job guard, decline → re-invite, the resend limit, revoke, change email) and the email's content and sender escaping. `specs/app-convex/invitations.spec.ts` runs the matchmaker and the candidate in two browser contexts and reads the links from the outbox (`apps/e2e/invite-emails.ts`).
- **Onboarding candidates (phase 1 step 3):** the workspace's **Onboard** button opens a form for the candidate's email, an optional name, social handles and the pasted DM history. Submitting writes, in one mutation (`candidates.mutations.onboard`), the candidate (`invited`, `active`), its conversation, the history as a private message at the start of the thread, an open invite, and the `candidate.created` / `invite.created` audit events. The matchmaker then lands in the new conversation. *Why:* this is how a candidate relationship moves from the DMs into the app.
  - **The same email twice** (any case, any membership state) writes nothing, and the form links to the existing candidate. Onboarding never reads `users`, so it behaves the same whether or not the email has an account (prd §9.1).
  - **Copyable invite links.** The token is HMAC-SHA256 of a per-invite nonce under a deployment secret (`INVITE_LINK_SECRET`, `convex/invites/helpers.ts`). Only the nonce and the token's hash are stored, so the database alone can't produce a working link, yet the owner can copy the same link again at any time (`invites.queries.token`). Set the secret once per deployment with `pnpm --filter @repo/api invites:setup [--prod]`; the e2e harness sets its own each run. *Why:* the spec stores only a hash but also wants **Copy invite link** at any time, and a hash alone can't rebuild the link.
  - **Handle rules** (`convex/candidates/rules.ts`, shared with the form): profile URLs and "@" are stripped for Instagram, TikTok and X. WhatsApp is stored as E.164 and must include the country code; a "(0)" trunk prefix and a leading "00" are handled, but a local number is refused, never guessed.
  - **Workspace:** the candidate list (most recent first, status filter, search by name or email, Invited/Declined/Left/Account deleted markers) and a read-only conversation view. Private messages are marked "Only visible to you". An invited candidate's conversation shows the invite banner with the link and **Copy invite link**. On a phone the conversation replaces the list, with a way back. A malformed or foreign conversation id shows "Conversation not found".
  - **UI:** `Textarea` and `NativeSelect` in `packages/ui`, both on Base UI's `Field.Control`.
  - **Tests:** convex-test suites for the rules, the invite tokens, onboarding (atomic writes, duplicate, no account lookup, tenant isolation, missing secret) and the list, conversation and token queries. Vitest for the app's label helpers, and `specs/app-convex/onboarding.spec.ts`. Shared e2e sign-up helpers now live in `apps/e2e/accounts.ts`.
- **Matchmaker profiles (phase 1 step 2):** any account can create a matchmaker profile at `/mm/new`, linked from the home page until the account owns one. *Why:* every later phase-1 step (onboarding, chat, the candidate panel) happens inside a matchmaker's workspace.
  - **Username rules** (`convex/matchmakers/rules.ts`, exported through `@repo/api` so the form checks exactly what the server does): 6–30 letters, digits and periods, no leading, trailing or doubled period, at least one letter, and the reserved list from `prd/phase-1.md` §1.2. Uniqueness is on the canonical key with dots removed, as in Gmail, so `jane.smith` is refused once `janesmith` exists.
  - **Backend:** `matchmakers.mutations.create` and `update` (display and business name; the username never changes), audited as `matchmaker.created` / `matchmaker.updated`. `matchmakers.queries.workspace` resolves a URL's username in any case or dot placement, and returns `null` alike for a profile that isn't yours and one that doesn't exist, so the URL can't be used to probe for usernames. `profileHistory` lists the profile's own audit events.
  - **App:** `/mm/:username` opens the workspace shell (candidate list and conversation column; a phone shows only the list). Other dot placements redirect to the chosen form. `/mm/:username/settings` edits the display and business names and shows the profile's history. The candidate panel and the Onboard button come with steps 3 and 6.
  - **Tests:** convex-test suites for the rules, mutations and queries (including a stranger being refused), Vitest for the app's history sentences and error helper, and `specs/app-convex/matchmaker-profile.spec.ts` with page objects in `page-objects/app/matchmaker.page.ts`.
- **Test accounts for the dev deployment (`convex/seed/dev/`):** `pnpm --filter @repo/api seed:dev` adds six `@matchmaker-dev.test` accounts: a matchmaker (Maya, owns `maya.matches`), three joined candidates (Sam has a short thread that includes a matchmaker-only message), one invited person, and one blank account. `seed:dev:code <email>` prints that address's latest sign-in code from the outbox. The seed only adds what's missing and never wipes, so it's safe to re-run. It refuses to run unless `SITE_URL` is on `.localhost` and `RESEND_API_KEY` is unset, so it can never touch prod. *Why:* dev had no data, and exercising the matchmaker, candidate and invite flows needed an account in each state.
- **Sign-in with Convex Auth (phase 1 step 1):** email + six-digit code, one flow for sign-in and sign-up (`convex/auth.ts`, `apps/app` `/sign-in`), then a name step for new accounts. A custom `createOrUpdateUser` (`users/helpers.ts`) keeps one account per email and never reuses a deleted account. Codes go out through Resend when `RESEND_API_KEY` is set; otherwise they're logged and written to an internal `emailOutbox` table, which is how local dev and the e2e suite sign in. `pnpm --filter @repo/api auth:setup` sets a deployment's signing keys. *Why:* the PRD moves auth from Clerk to Convex Auth and uses codes rather than magic links, which break in installed home-screen apps.
- **Phase 1 schema and foundations (`packages/api/convex/`):** every phase-1 table (users with soft deletion, matchmakers, candidates (which also carry the open invitation), conversations, messages, notes, audit events, notification tables). Also the access helpers `requireUser`, `requireMatchmaker` + `assertSameTenant` and `requireCandidateSelf`, and the append-only audit trail (`audit/rules.ts` action list, `recordAudit`). `users.setName` is audited at account level and fanned out to each joined matchmaker's trail. *Why:* every later step builds on these, and tenancy and audit are easier to enforce from day one than to retrofit.
- **App shell (`apps/app`):** React Router, a sign-in gate on every route but `/sign-in` (with a safe `?next=` return path), and a home page listing invitations, owned matchmaker profiles and joined matchmakers. Vitest for app utilities.
- **Shared UI primitives (`packages/ui`):** design tokens (`@repo/ui/theme.css`, the marketing site's palette) and Base UI–based `Button`, `Field`/`Input`, `CodeField` (OTP) and `Card`.
- **E2E auth support (`apps/e2e`):** global setup gives the local backend fresh auth keys each run, specs read codes from the outbox (`sign-in-codes.ts`), and there are new sign-in, sign-up and home specs with page objects. See `docs/e2e-architecture.md` §1d.
- **Marketing site for Matchmaker (`apps/www`):** replaces the turbostack template page with a landing page built from the PRD — hero with an illustrated product still (a client thread showing an auto-applied fact and a suggested reply), the problem today, how it works (paste the DM → approve the first email → one thread), six features, the "AI drafts, you decide" principles, privacy and tenant isolation, a FAQ (Base UI Accordion) and a waitlist form. New warm ivory/claret palette in `globals.css` tokens (light and dark) and an Instrument Serif display face (`--font-display`). *Why:* the site described a monorepo template, not the product. Pricing is left out on purpose: the PRD defers billing, so the FAQ says pricing comes later.
- **Waitlist backend (`packages/api/convex/waitlist/`):** a `waitlist` table (indexed `by_email`) and a public `waitlist.mutations.join` mutation that validates, normalises and dedupes on email. It returns `null` whether or not the address was already on the list, so the endpoint can't be used to check who signed up, and a repeat sign-up only fills in fields that were blank. `internal.waitlist.queries.list` pages sign-ups for the dashboard/CLI; nothing public reads them. Validation rules live in `waitlist/rules.ts` with no Convex imports and are re-exported from `@repo/api`, so the form and the server enforce the same limits. The form also has a honeypot field and a 15s submit timeout, so it can't spin forever while the backend is unreachable.

Runs in Vitest's `node` environment: convex-test's recommended `@edge-runtime/vm` peer made pnpm install a second copy of vitest, which broke `@repo/ui`'s typecheck.

### Changed
- **One Doppler project for every app:** `matchmaker_app` and `matchmaker_www` are merged into a single `matchmaker` project, with one branch config per app under each environment (`dev_app`, `dev_admin`, `dev_www`). `doppler.yaml` binds each app directory to its config, and `apps/e2e/doppler.ts` now takes the app name and builds `<environment>_<app>`; `E2E_DOPPLER_CONFIG` is replaced by `E2E_DOPPLER_ENVIRONMENT`. *Why:* the Doppler workplace is at its project limit, and a project per app would not leave room for the admin app. Values shared by every app can now live in the root `dev` config and be inherited. Rerun `doppler setup --no-interactive` in every checkout and worktree after pulling.
- **Sign-in emails come from `no-reply@aileenlancif.com`** (was `no-reply@matchmaker.io`). *Why:* Resend only sends from a verified domain, and `matchmaker.io` isn't ours. `aileenlancif.com` is the interim production domain and is verified in Resend.
- **`pnpm dev` serves both frontends from `https://matchmaker.localhost`:** the marketing site at `/` and the app at `/app/`, mirroring production's one-origin layout (before: `www.matchmaker.localhost` and `app.matchmaker.localhost/app/`). Portless routes by hostname only, so the app's Vite server now runs as `matchmaker` and, only under portless (`PORTLESS_URL`), proxies every path outside `/app` to the Next dev server at `www.matchmaker.localhost`, including Next's HMR websocket. Bare `/app` redirects to `/app/`, and `matchmaker.localhost` joins Next's `allowedDevOrigins`. *Why:* the site's `/app/` links now work in dev, and dev shares cookies and storage the way production does. The e2e suite is unchanged: it runs Vite without portless, so no proxy.
- **Marketing site (`apps/www`) rewritten against the phase-1 spec (`prd/`):** *Why:* the site described the old single-file PRD — email threads, per-matchmaker sending domains, AI drafting the first email — none of which is in phase 1.
  - **How it works** is now the invitation flow: the matchmaker clicks Onboard and enters the candidate's email (name, social handles and the pasted DM history optional; the history is visible only to them) → the candidate gets an invitation from `invites@matchmaker.io` or a shareable invite link → they sign up, accept, and the conversation continues in the app's chat.
  - **Email is no longer described as a conversation channel.** Gone: reply-by-email, "one thread across channels" and "sent from your domain" (feature card and FAQ). A new FAQ answer says email only carries sign-in codes, invitations and content-free new-message notifications.
  - **"Clients" are now "candidates"** throughout the copy.
  - **AI is framed as coming next.** The six feature cards now cover only phase 1 (one chat per candidate, imported DM history, invite by email or link, notes and change history, notifications, mobile), and suggested replies and the self-building profile move to a dashed "Coming next" callout (`data-testid="coming-next"`). The principles ("We organise. You decide.") and FAQ are rewritten the same way.
  - **Hero illustration** no longer shows an auto-applied fact or a suggested reply. It shows the pasted Instagram history marked "Only visible to you", the invitation being accepted, the chat and composer, and the candidate panel's History tab.
  - **Privacy copy** drops "only used to find matches" (phase 3) and adds that nothing is deleted: the matchmaker keeps the conversation and notes if a candidate leaves or deletes their account, and candidates are told so before accepting.
  - Removed the leftover `/.clerk/` entry from `apps/www/.gitignore` (auth is Convex Auth).
  - e2e: `page-objects/www/landing.page.ts` gains `getComingNext()`. `specs/www/landing.spec.ts` updates the mock and FAQ assertions and adds tests for the invitation flow and for keeping AI out of the feature cards.
- **HTTP routing (`convex/http.ts`, `convex.config.ts`):** the app's router now owns the whole `.convex.site` URL space. Auth discovery is at `/.well-known/…`, HTTP actions at `/api/…`, and the two static sites are registered as catch-alls (`/app/…`, then `/`) instead of being mounted with `httpPrefix`. The health check moved to `/api/health` (same URL as before). The app's Vite `base` is now a fixed `/app/` rather than `STATIC_HOSTING_BASE_PATH`. *Why:* Convex validates Convex Auth's tokens through an OpenID discovery document that has to sit at the site root, and the `customJwt` alternative rejects tokens without a `kid` header.
- **Interim production domain: `www.aileenlancif.com`.** The production deployment will be served there with the existing path-based layout (`/`, `/app/`, `/api/`), and the apex redirects to `www`. PRD §10 records the decision and README → *Custom domain* has the DNS and Convex steps. *Why:* the PRD's `matchmaker.io` is taken and no product domain has been bought yet; this gets a real HTTPS origin now without blocking that choice.
- **e2e runs in several worktrees at once without colliding** (`docs/e2e-architecture.md` §1c). Parallel agents each work in their own git worktree and may all run the suite at the same time. Each run now brings up its own Vite, Next and Convex backend, and nothing is shared between them:
  - **Convex ports are allocated per run** and passed to the CLI with `--local-cloud-port` / `--local-site-port`. `scripts/convex-local.mjs` writes them into the worktree's `config.json` before starting. Before, a worktree's recorded port could be held by a sibling's backend. Every anonymous deployment is named `anonymous-agent`, so the CLI mistook the sibling for its own backend and refused to start.
  - **All ports come from 20000–32000, via `scripts/pick-ports.mjs`, instead of `listen(0)`.** Port 0 draws from the OS's ephemeral range, which outgoing connections also use. With two suites running, `next dev` hit `EADDRINUSE` on a port the allocator had just released. Next also binds `127.0.0.1` explicitly now.
  - **One backend per worktree** (`packages/api/.convex/e2e-backend.lock`). A second run in the same checkout fails straight away instead of starting a second backend on the same SQLite file or wiping the database mid-run. The Convex webServer is no longer reused (`reuseExistingServer: false`). A stale lock left by a killed run is taken over once its holder is confirmed dead.
  - Verified with two worktrees, one with no deployment yet: three rounds of both suites at once, 21/21 each time. Also tested: both worktrees recording the same port (both passed) and two runs in one worktree (the second was refused, the first passed).
- **e2e app servers run under Doppler:** `apps/e2e/doppler.ts` wraps the Vite and Next `webServer` commands in `doppler run --project matchmaker_{app,www} --config dev`, so the suite gets the same env as `pnpm dev`. Variables the harness sets itself (`VITE_CONVEX_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_CONVEX_URL`, `NEXT_DIST_DIR`) are passed to `--preserve-env`. By default Doppler would override them with the *cloud* Convex URL, and the suite would then talk to your cloud deployment while seeding the local one. `E2E_DOPPLER=0` runs the servers without Doppler (CI sets it: CI has no Doppler token), and `E2E_DOPPLER_CONFIG` selects a different config.
- **The e2e `www` server now gets a Convex URL from the harness**, the same local or placeholder URL as `apps/app`. Before, it relied on `apps/www/.env.local`: a fresh checkout failed env validation, and a filled-in one pointed the site under test at a cloud deployment.
- **`apps/www` is a static export:** `output: "export"`, `trailingSlash: true`, unoptimised images, and `manifest.ts` marked `force-static`. The Clerk middleware (`proxy.ts`), Clerk provider and sign-in/up buttons are gone — a static site has no server to run them — and "Get Started"/"Sign In" now link to `/app/`, where auth will live.
- **`apps/app` builds with `base: "/app/"`** (overridable by the static-hosting CLI's `STATIC_HOSTING_BASE_PATH`); the service worker registers from `BASE_URL` and caches `./` rather than `/`. The e2e `app` projects navigate to `/app/`.

- **`apps/app` uses `@vitejs/plugin-react` instead of `@vitejs/plugin-react-swc`:** *Why:* Vite 8 (Rolldown) warns that the SWC plugin is slower when no SWC plugins are configured, and none are; `plugin-react` does Fast Refresh through Oxc there. Build, typecheck and e2e unchanged.

### Fixed
- **Blank page at `/app` without a trailing slash in production:** the app's router basename was `/app/`, which the bare `/app` doesn't match, and production serves that path without redirecting (dev redirects it, so this only showed up in prod). The basename is now `/app`, which matches both.
- **`convex dev` / `convex deploy` failed typechecking `convex/auth.config.ts`** (`Cannot find name 'process'`). Convex typechecks `convex/` without Node's types, so the file now reads `CONVEX_SITE_URL` through the generated, typed `env` from `_generated/server` (which is `process.env` at runtime), like the rest of the backend.
